The Fall 2025 Unified Agenda is 130 days overdue.

The federal government is legally required to publish the Unified Agenda of regulatory actions twice a year. The Fall 2025 edition was due by December 31, 2025. It has not been published.

Why this matters

The Unified Agenda is the public's main window into what regulations federal agencies are working on. Without it, the public, the press, and regulated industries are left guessing about which rules are coming, which have been quietly shelved, and which are years behind schedule.

Publication is required by Executive Order 12866 and the Regulatory Flexibility Act (5 U.S.C. § 602). Both require semiannual publication.

The pattern is broader than one missing edition. The Spring 2025 edition was itself published on September 4, 2025 — three to five months past its historical window. Late publication has become routine.

Publication cadence, 2010–today

Each bar shows how many days passed between consecutive Unified Agenda editions. The legally implied cadence is twice a year (roughly 180 days). The final red bar is the time elapsed since the most recent edition (Spring 2025, published September 22, 2025), and it's still growing.

Rules currently under OIRA review

While Executive Order 12866 gives OIRA up to 120 days (90 days plus a 30 day extension) to review regulations, many rules blow past this deadline. When that happens, they are either undergoing extended review, quietly shelved, or trapped in a loop between the agency and OIRA. Unfortunately, reginfo.gov doesn't differentiate between these situations because they all just show up as "Pending Review."

About this tracker

This page surfaces publicly available signals about US federal regulatory activity, information that is technically open on reginfo.gov but functionally inaccessible there.

Two signals are visible above. The first is the lateness of the Unified Agenda, the semiannual document where federal agencies declare which rules they're working on. The second is the queue of rules currently under OIRA review (the executive branch gate every significant regulation passes through) with each rule's clock now visible and searchable.

More signals are planned: rules OIRA has quietly withdrawn or returned during review, the EO 12866 meeting log (regulatory-side lobbying made visible), and a per-rule view that ties together the Unified Agenda, OIRA review, and Federal Register publication.
